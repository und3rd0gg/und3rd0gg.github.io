Privacy policy

Updated 30 October 2026 · Publisher: underdogg

Notes on your device

UnderdoggNote keeps Ephemeral (temporary) notes, local recovery data and settings on your device. Notes you save to Obsidian are ordinary UTF-8 Markdown files in the vault you pick, and in a subfolder if you set one. The app doesn't add its own encryption to these files or to its note databases.

On Android, the note browser widget doesn't show note text by default. If you turn previews on, your launcher and anyone who can see your home screen can see that text. Previews use a small local cache that isn't included in Android backup. Turning previews off clears the cache and replaces the widget text with a neutral message.

On Android, working with a vault on the file system can require the system permission to access all shared files. That's broader than one folder. The app uses it for your configured vault and file operations, and the system permission screen shows exactly what you're granting.

Deleting a note removes it from your current notes, but earlier text can remain in sync history, conflict copies, local archives, exported files or operating-system backups. Closing or uninstalling the app doesn't necessarily erase all those copies.

Microsoft Store

Microsoft runs the Microsoft Store and handles the data that comes with its services. UnderdoggNote doesn't collect payment card details. See Microsoft’s privacy statement.

Google sync (optional)

Sync stays off unless you turn it on. To connect your account, the app uses its Google account ID and email address, with your permission. It asks for these scopes: openid, email and https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata. They don't give access to your regular Google Drive files, contacts, Gmail or photos.

What syncs: Ephemeral note text and its revision history, deletion markers, account and platform settings, and device details such as installation IDs, device names, platform and sync status. All of it is stored in the app's private appDataFolder on your Google Drive and shared between your connected installations.

What doesn't sync: vault files, local file paths, window size and position, the selected note and the launch-at-login setting. Your operating system or a vault sync service you choose can handle those files separately.

The app talks to Google over HTTPS. Sync isn't end-to-end encrypted: Google stores the synced data, and any installation you've authorized can read it. Note text isn't sent to a server run by the developer and isn't used for advertising or AI training.

Sign-in

On Windows and macOS you sign in through your system browser, and credentials are kept in the operating system's credential store. On Android, Google Play Services handles authorization: short-lived access tokens stay in the native part of the app, and a private link between your account ID and email lets the app ask for the same account again. Tokens aren't passed to the WebView that shows your notes. Google and the operating-system makers have their own services and data practices.

Disconnecting Google stops sync in this app and clears its current sign-in state. Your local notes and recovery data stay. The cloud library isn't deleted, and the access you granted to the whole Google project isn't revoked; you can manage that in your Google account.

Delete cloud sync data, once you confirm, removes content and portable settings from retired sync generations. A small amount of reset metadata can remain so that an old installation that has been offline can't quietly upload the old history again. Local archives, exports and copies on your other installations have to be handled separately. If you deliberately rejoin, locally saved text can be uploaded to the new library.

On Windows, the interface runs on Microsoft Edge WebView2. This Microsoft runtime can collect diagnostic data and includes Microsoft Defender SmartScreen, which can send security-related information to Microsoft. See the Microsoft Privacy Statement and SmartScreen privacy information. These Microsoft components have their own terms and practices.

Other network requests and sharing

For currency math, the app can download a public table of USD exchange rates from cdn.jsdelivr.net or latest.currency-api.pages.dev. These requests don't include note text, expressions or a Google token. Like any network request, they reveal network details such as your IP address to that service.

When you export, move, share or open an external link, the file, app, storage provider or browser you chose takes it from there. The app doesn't control how long they keep data or how they handle it.

The app doesn't automatically upload sync telemetry. Its built-in sync diagnostic log records event times and results. Exporting diagnostics saves a file wherever you choose; on Android you can use any document app on your device. The app doesn't send that file to the developer by itself. Check what's in it before you share it.

Operating-system backup and restore

Android app-data backup or moving to a new device can keep your notes and settings, depending on your system settings. Encrypted credentials, device-specific Google account links and the restore marker are left out of backup. An external Obsidian vault isn't part of this backup either. After a restore, the installation gets a new sync identity and the restored notes stay.
